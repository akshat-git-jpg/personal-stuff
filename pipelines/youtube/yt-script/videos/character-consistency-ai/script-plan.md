#### 3.1 · InVideo AI

**SAY**
> "InVideo AI finishes last here on pure consistency — in this test, the character's face and outfit shifted noticeably between scenes. Worth flagging: InVideo's own newer materials describe a 'persistent character sheet' system aimed directly at this problem, which sounds like a real answer to what we saw fail. I haven't tested that version myself, so take the ranking here as what this specific test showed, not a permanent verdict on the platform."

#### 3.2 · Midjourney

**SAY**
> "Midjourney is still the best-looking single image of the five, by a clear margin. But it was never built to carry a character across a moving story — no native video, and even on stills, aggressive angles start to drift the face. Best tool here for concept art. Not the tool for a multi-scene story."

#### 3.3 · Atlabs

**SAY**
> "Atlabs is the easy middle ground — simple storyboard casting, clean lip-sync, a real all-in-one pipeline. It just doesn't hold the character through a big environment or action change. Good for straightforward marketing clips. Not built for anything visually ambitious."

#### 3.4 · Higgsfield

**SAY**
> "Higgsfield does one thing better than anyone else here — Soul ID locks the actual face through wild camera angles better than any other platform in this test. The gap is everything around the face — wardrobe drifts unless you fight it in every prompt. The best tool if faces and motion are your priority and you're willing to manage the wardrobe by hand."

#### 3.5 · OpenArt — the winner

**SAY**
> "And OpenArt wins this comparison. Not because any single image is the most beautiful — Midjourney still takes that. It wins because it's the only platform that kept the face, the hair, the sweater, and the pendant recognizable across all five scenes, including a full lighting change, and then carried that same character into video without an extra tool. Character Builder, a saved library, a real LoRA, and native video generation, all in one place. That's the actual job this video was testing, and OpenArt is the only one that did it end to end."
