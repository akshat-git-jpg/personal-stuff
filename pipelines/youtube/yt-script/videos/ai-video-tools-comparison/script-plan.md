# Best AI Video Software — Pictory vs InVideo vs Fliki vs Synthesia vs Lumen5

## Intro

If you're trying to pick an AI video tool — for a YouTube channel, for your
business, or just for posting on social — this is the video that settles it.

I've spent the last few months actually making videos in all five of these.
Not five-minute demos, not trailers. Real projects, real deadlines, and I paid
for the plans myself. So everything here comes from using them, not from
reading their landing pages.

We'll go through it in five parts: the features each tool actually gives you,
the honest pros and cons, what their customer support is like when something
breaks, the full pricing picture, and then my straight answer on which one you
should pick.

If you want to try any of these yourself, there are free-trial links for all
five in the description.

Alright, let's get into it.

## Body

### 1. Features

- **A real faceless video that's making money** — show a finished channel doing
  big numbers before touching any UI. This is the payoff; make them want it
  before you explain how it works.
- **Pictory — dashboard** — lead with how little there is to learn; this is the
  one a non-technical person can open cold.
- **Pictory — Script to Video** — paste a script, pick a template, set aspect
  ratio, get a video. Mention a script can come straight out of ChatGPT.
- **Pictory — Article to Video** — same engine, URL instead of text. Frame it
  as the blog-repurposing path.
- **Pictory — Edit Video Using Text** — this one's for footage you already have,
  not generation. Call that distinction out clearly.
- **Pictory — Visual to Video** — drag in images or clips. Quickest to skim.
- **InVideo — dashboard** — similar cleanliness to Pictory; don't oversell the
  difference, the gap shows up later in the features.
- **InVideo — template library** — the volume here is the whole argument. Show
  the scroll, not one template.
- **InVideo — AI text to video** — prompt in, script *and* video out. This is the
  one feature almost nobody else has; give it the most time in this section.
- **InVideo — stock media** — frame as raw material quality, keep it short.
- **InVideo — editor** — swap footage, edit text, highlight sections. Show one
  real edit rather than listing options.
- **Fliki — dashboard** — be honest that it's the least intuitive of the five,
  then immediately say it's an afternoon to learn, not a wall.
- **Fliki — core video features** — cover fast; the point is that it matches
  Pictory and InVideo rather than beating them.
- **Fliki — voice cloning** — the reason to care about Fliki at all. Play a
  cloned sample; nothing else in this lineup does it.
- **Synthesia — dashboard** — set up that this is a different category of tool,
  not another faceless-video generator.
- **Synthesia — avatar library** — scroll the range, then customise one on
  camera. Appearance, voice, expressions.
- **Synthesia — languages and accents** — the international angle; this is what
  sells it to business buyers.
- **Synthesia — custom avatars and gestures** — the "make it look like you" path.
  Flag now that gesture range is thin — you'll pay it off in the cons.
- **Lumen5 — dashboard** — friendly, and that's genuinely its strength.
- **Lumen5 — blog to video** — same idea as Pictory's script-to-video, but be
  upfront it's the weaker version and say why.
- **Lumen5 — talking head** — the Synthesia comparison is unavoidable here. Draw
  it directly and don't soften it.

> **Verdict:** Every one of these will get you a video. Pictory and InVideo are
> competing for the same faceless-video job, Fliki is the voice specialist,
> Synthesia is playing a different sport, and Lumen5 is doing two things at once
> without doing either as well.

### 2. Pros & Cons

- **Pictory — filler-word removal** — the time-saver nobody advertises. Lead with
  it, it's the most underrated thing in this video.
- **Pictory — stock footage matching** — the strongest technical claim in the
  lineup. Show it picking the right clip from a script line.
- **Pictory — voiceover realism** — play it against a weaker one so the gap is
  audible, not asserted.
- **Pictory — team collaboration** — only matters to the org buyer; keep it brief.
- **Pictory — the two real cons** — thin short-form templates and the price. Say
  both plainly; the honesty is what makes the recommendation land later.
- **InVideo — affordability** — the headline advantage. Don't bury it.
- **InVideo — short-form template volume** — pair it with the affordability point;
  together they're the whole case for InVideo.
- **InVideo — round-the-clock support** — small point, real one for beginners.
- **InVideo — voiceover quality** — the honest trade. It's the price you pay for
  the price you pay.
- **InVideo — footage matching accuracy** — the one that actually costs you time.
  Show a mismatch and how long fixing it takes.
- **Fliki — voice cloning quality** — the reason it exists. Reinforce, don't repeat.
- **Fliki — brand customisation** — logo, fonts, colours. Aim this at the business
  viewer specifically.
- **Fliki — price against its peers** — the problem. State it before the credits.
- **Fliki — the credit system** — explain why credits are confusing to budget
  against compared to a flat video count.
- **Synthesia — avatar range and customisation** — the strongest thing here; show
  breadth rather than describing it.
- **Synthesia — voice languages** — reinforce the international case.
- **Synthesia — expression and gesture limits** — the cons you flagged earlier.
  Show a clip where it reads slightly robotic and let the viewer judge.
- **Lumen5 — what it genuinely does well** — covering two formats, decent templates.
  Give it a fair hearing before the cons.
- **Lumen5 — no AI voiceover** — the dealbreaker. Explain what that actually means
  for your workflow, don't just state the absence.
- **Lumen5 — the length ceiling** — the cap doesn't move no matter what you pay.
  This is the point that ends the argument.

> **Verdict:** Pictory buys you quality, InVideo buys you volume for less, Fliki
> buys you one exceptional feature at a premium, and Synthesia buys you a
> presenter. Lumen5's limits aren't tier limits — they're product limits.

### 3. Customer Support

- **Email-only tools** — say which, and be clear about what email-only means when
  you're mid-project and stuck.
- **Live chat tools** — the meaningful upgrade. Explain why chat matters most when
  you're new to a tool.
- **Phone support** — call out who offers it and be fair that it's a real perk.

> **Verdict:** Support quality tracks almost inversely with everything else here —
> the tool with the best support line isn't the tool I'd recommend.

### 4. Pricing

- **How I'm splitting this** — three buckets: long-form faceless, short-form, and
  talking-head. Say this out loud; it's what stops the section becoming a wall
  of numbers.
- **Long-form — the free plans** — go tool by tool. The key thing to land: one of
  these free tiers won't let you download what you make.
- **Pictory — the three tiers** — walk who each is for, not just what each costs.
- **InVideo — the two tiers plus the guarantee** — the money-back window is worth
  a sentence of its own.
- **Fliki — the two tiers** — translate credits into videos out loud so it's
  comparable to everything else on screen.
- **Lumen5 — the four tiers** — the point isn't the prices, it's that the ceiling
  never lifts. Say it once, clearly.
- **Long-form, like for like** — put the same monthly output through the two real
  contenders and show the gap. This is the most important comparison in the video.
- **Short-form** — only three tools qualify. Compare template count against price
  and the answer falls out on its own.
- **Talking head — the free options** — one trial, one forever-free plan; explain
  what each actually restricts.
- **Talking head — the paid plans** — compare the cheapest plan of one against the
  near-top plan of the other. That contrast is the whole argument.
- **The yearly-plan tip** — worth saying before you leave pricing.

> **Verdict:** InVideo on cost for faceless work, Synthesia on value for
> talking-head. Fliki and Lumen5 are both asking premium money for a narrower
> product.

### 5. Which One Should You Pick

- **Best quality, long-form faceless** — name it and give the two reasons that
  earned it.
- **Tight budget, long-form faceless** — name it and be explicit this is not a
  downgrade for most people.
- **You need voice cloning** — name it; single-feature buy, and say so.
- **Short-form and Reels** — name it, and point at the template volume as the
  reason.
- **Talking head or AI presenter** — name it, and note the language range as the
  tiebreaker.

> **Verdict:** There's no single winner here, and anyone telling you otherwise is
> selling something. Match the tool to the format you're actually making.
